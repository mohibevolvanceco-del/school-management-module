Lesson 3: Leading, Communicating and Using Data

Leading people

No manager can do everything alone. Good leaders delegate: they hand each task to a suitable person, explain clearly what’s needed, and check in without taking over. They also keep staff motivated by recognizing good work and supporting anyone who’s struggling, which matters just as much as handing out the right tasks.

Handling a disagreement

  1. Listen to each person separately and calmly.
  2. Separate the facts from the opinions.
  3. Look for a solution that’s fair to everyone and follows school rules.
  4. Agree on next steps, then follow up later.

Communicating clearly

Even the right message can fail if it goes through the wrong channel. A good manager picks the channel to fit the audience and how urgent the message is:

MessageAudienceBest channel
School closing early tomorrowAll parentsParents’ WhatsApp group or SMS alert
New exam scheduleStaff and studentsShared calendar and notice board
Concern about one studentOne familyPrivate phone call or meeting
Ideas for improving the canteenStudents and parentsShort Google Form survey
New policy explainedTeachersStaff meeting, followed by an email summary

Using data to make fair decisions

Schools collect a lot of information: attendance, results, fee records and survey answers. A good manager uses it to spot problems early, like a class whose attendance drops every Monday, or a subject where many students are failing. Data shows where to look. Then the manager talks to people to find out why.

Keep it private

Only staff who need student data should see it.

Keep it safe

Use personal passwords and back up records.

Keep it fair

Use data to help students, never to embarrass them.

Discuss: A teacher wants to post the full class result list, with names, in a public group. What should the manager say?
