Lesson 3: Leading, Communicating and Using Data

Leading people

A manager cannot do everything alone. Good leaders delegate: they give each task to a suitable person, explain clearly what is needed, and check in without taking over. They also motivate staff by recognising good work and supporting those who are struggling.

Handling a disagreement

  1. Listen to each person separately and calmly.
  2. Find the facts, not just opinions.
  3. Look for a solution that is fair to everyone and follows school rules.
  4. Agree on next steps and follow up later.

Communicating clearly

The right message can fail if it goes through the wrong channel. A good manager chooses the channel to fit the audience and how urgent the message is:

MessageAudienceBest channel
School closing early tomorrowAll parentsParents’ WhatsApp group or SMS alert
New exam scheduleStaff and studentsShared calendar and notice board
Concern about one studentOne familyPrivate phone call or meeting
Ideas for improving the canteenStudents and parentsShort Google Form survey
New policy explainedTeachersStaff meeting, followed by an email summary

Using data to make fair decisions

Schools collect a lot of information: attendance, results, fee records and survey answers. A good manager uses it to spot problems early, for example a class whose attendance drops every Monday, or a subject where many students are failing. The data shows where to look, and the manager then talks to people to understand why.

Keep it private

Only staff who need student data should see it.

Keep it safe

Use personal passwords and back up records.

Keep it fair

Use data to help students, not to embarrass them.

Discuss: a teacher wants to post the full class result list, with names, in a public group. What should the manager say?
