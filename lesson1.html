Lesson 1: The School Manager’s Role

What does a school manager do?

A school manager, usually the principal, head teacher or a coordinator, is responsible for making sure the school works well for everyone. Teachers focus on their own classes, but the manager looks after the whole school: staff, students, parents, timetables, money and buildings.

Most of a manager’s work falls into four jobs that repeat throughout the year:

Figure: the four jobs of a school manager repeat in a cycle all year.

Plan

Decide what the school wants to achieve and how. Example: setting the term calendar and exam dates.

Organise

Arrange people, time and money to carry out the plan. Example: making the timetable and duty roster.

Lead

Guide and motivate staff and students. Example: running staff meetings and supporting new teachers.

Monitor

Check how things are going and improve them. Example: reviewing results and attendance each month.

Qualities of a good school manager

QualityWhat it looks like in practice
OrganisedKeeps a clear calendar, meets deadlines and plans ahead
FairTreats all staff and students by the same rules
Good communicatorGives clear messages and listens to concerns
Calm under pressureSolves problems step by step instead of panicking
Open to new ideasTries useful tools and better ways of working
ResponsibleProtects students’ safety and private information

Where technology fits in

Technology does not make someone a good manager, but it helps a good manager save time and stay organised. Throughout this module you will see simple, free tools, such as shared calendars, spreadsheets and online forms, used for real management tasks.

Think about it: think of a teacher or principal you respect. Which of the six qualities above do they show most?
