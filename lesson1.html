Lesson 1: The School Manager’s Role

What does a school manager do?

A school manager, usually the principal, head teacher or a coordinator, makes sure the school works well for everyone. Teachers focus on their own classes. The manager looks after everything else that keeps a school running, from staff, students and parents to timetables, money and buildings.

Most of a manager’s work falls into four jobs that repeat all year:

Figure: the four jobs of a school manager repeat in a cycle all year.

Plan

Decide what the school wants to achieve and how. Example: setting the term calendar and exam dates.

Organize

Arrange people, time and money to carry out the plan. Example: making the timetable and duty roster.

Lead

Guide and motivate staff and students. Example: running staff meetings and supporting new teachers.

Monitor

Check how things are going and improve them. Example: reviewing results and attendance each month.

Qualities of a good school manager

QualityWhat it looks like
OrganizedKeeps a clear calendar, meets deadlines and plans ahead
FairHolds all staff and students to the same rules
Good communicatorGives clear messages and listens to concerns
Calm under pressureSolves problems step by step instead of panicking
Open to new ideasTries useful tools and better ways of working
ResponsibleProtects students’ safety and private information

Where technology fits in

Tools don’t make good managers. What they do is save a good manager time and keep the work organized. Throughout this module, you’ll see simple, free tools like shared calendars, spreadsheets and online forms used for real school tasks, from booking meetings to tracking a budget.

Think about it: Picture a teacher or principal you respect. Which of the six qualities above do they show most?
