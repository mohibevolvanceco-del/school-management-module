Lesson 2: Planning and Organizing

Planning: deciding what and when

Good planning starts with clear goals. “Improve results” is too vague, because nobody can check it. A good manager writes something checkable instead, like “raise the Grade 9 science pass rate from 70% to 80% by the final exam,” and then plans the steps, the people involved and the dates.

Organizing: people, time and money

Once there’s a plan, the manager organizes the resources to carry it out. Here are common school tasks and the simple tools that help with each:

TaskToolWhy it helps
Term calendarGoogle CalendarEveryone sees the same dates, and updates appear at once
Duty roster and substitute listGoogle SheetsEasy to edit and share, and shows who’s free at a glance
Event budgetGoogle Sheets or ExcelAdds up costs automatically and shows the money left
Staff documents and policiesShared Google Drive folderOne place for the latest version, so no files get lost
Team task listA shared to-do list, like Google Keep or TrelloShows who’s doing what and what’s finished

Example: organizing a Parent-Teacher Meeting

  1. Plan: pick a date in Google Calendar that doesn’t clash with exams.
  2. Organize: make a sheet of time slots for each teacher and share it with staff.
  3. Inform: send parents the date and a Google Form to book a slot.
  4. Prepare: ask teachers to update results so they’re ready to share.
  5. Review: after the meeting, collect quick feedback from parents with a short form.
Remember: A tool is only as good as the plan behind it. A shared calendar with the wrong dates helps no one.
