Lesson 2: Planning and Organising

Planning: deciding what and when

Good planning starts with clear goals. Instead of “improve results”, a good manager writes a goal that can be checked, such as “raise the Grade 9 science pass rate from 70% to 80% by the final exam”. Then they plan the steps, the people involved and the dates.

Organising: people, time and money

Once there is a plan, the manager organises the resources to carry it out. Here are common school tasks and simple tools that help:

TaskHelpful toolWhy it helps
Term calendar (exams, holidays, events)Google CalendarEveryone sees the same dates, and updates appear at once
Duty roster and substitute listGoogle SheetsEasy to edit and share, and shows who is free at a glance
Event budgetGoogle Sheets or ExcelAdds up costs automatically and shows money left
Staff documents and policiesShared Google Drive folderOne place for the latest version, with no lost files
Task list for a teamA shared to-do list, such as Google Keep or TrelloShows who is doing what and what is finished

Example: organising a Parent-Teacher Meeting

  1. Plan: choose a date in Google Calendar that does not clash with exams.
  2. Organise: make a sheet of time slots for each teacher and share it with staff.
  3. Inform: send parents the date and a Google Form to book a slot.
  4. Prepare: ask teachers to update results so they are ready to share.
  5. Review: after the meeting, collect quick feedback from parents with a short form.
Remember: a tool is only as good as the plan behind it. A shared calendar with the wrong dates helps no one.
